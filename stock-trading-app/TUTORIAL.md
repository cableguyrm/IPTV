# Stock Trading for Complete Beginners

This tutorial assumes you know nothing about stocks. Each lesson is short. Read them in order, and try things in the app's **Practice** mode as you go. Practice uses pretend money, so you can't lose anything.

> **Important:** This is education, not financial advice. Investing in stocks can lose money, including money you put in. Never invest money you need for rent, bills, food, or emergencies.

## 1. What is a stock?

Imagine your friend opens a pizza shop. She needs $10,000 to get started, so she splits the shop into **100 equal pieces** and sells them for **$100 each**. You buy 5 pieces for $500.

You now own **5% of the pizza shop**. Each of those pieces is called a **share**, and owning shares is owning **stock**.

- If the shop does well and people want to buy pieces, a piece might become worth $150. Your 5 pieces are now worth $750. You've **gained $250**.
- If the shop does badly, a piece might drop to $60. Your 5 pieces are now worth $300. You've **lost $200** (on paper, until you sell).

Big companies like Apple, Coca-Cola and Nike work the same way, except they're split into **billions** of shares, and millions of people buy and sell them every day.

**Key idea:** A share is a tiny piece of ownership in a real company.

## 2. Why do stock prices go up and down?

A share's price is simply **the last price someone was willing to pay for it**. It changes all day because buyers and sellers keep agreeing on new prices.

Prices go **up** when more people want to buy than sell, for example when:

- The company reports bigger profits than expected
- It launches a popular new product
- The whole economy looks strong

Prices go **down** when more people want to sell, for example when:

- Profits are worse than expected
- There's bad news (a scandal, a lawsuit, a recall)
- People are worried about the economy

In the short term (days, weeks), prices move a lot for reasons that are hard to predict, even for professionals. Over the long term (many years), prices tend to follow how well the company actually does.

**Key idea:** Nobody can reliably predict tomorrow's price. Be suspicious of anyone who says they can.

## 3. Where do you buy stocks? Markets, brokers and tickers

- **Stock market / exchange:** The marketplace where shares are bought and sold. Famous US exchanges are the **NYSE** and the **NASDAQ**.
- **Broker:** You can't walk into the exchange yourself. A broker is a company that places your buy and sell orders for you. This app uses a broker called **Alpaca**.
- **Ticker symbol:** A short code for each company. Instead of typing "Apple Inc." you type **AAPL**.

| Company | Ticker |
|---|---|
| Apple | AAPL |
| Microsoft | MSFT |
| Coca-Cola | KO |
| Tesla | TSLA |
| S&P 500 fund (500 big US companies in one) | SPY |

- **Market hours:** The main US market is open **Monday to Friday, 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM New York time**, closed on holidays. If you place an order while it's closed, it waits until the market opens. (Practice mode is open 24/7.)

**Key idea:** You pick a ticker, tell your broker what to do, and the broker trades on the exchange for you.

## 4. How you make (or lose) money

There are two ways a stock can earn you money:

1. **Price going up (a "capital gain").** Buy low, sell higher.
2. **Dividends.** Some companies share part of their profits with shareholders, often every 3 months. Coca-Cola is famous for this.

**Worked example:**

- You buy **10 shares** of a company at **$50** each. Cost: 10 × $50 = **$500**.
- Months later the price is **$58**. Your shares are worth 10 × $58 = **$580**.
- Your gain is $580 − $500 = **$80**, which is $80 ÷ $500 = **16%**.

If the price had dropped to $42 instead, you'd have 10 × $42 = $420, a **loss of $80 (−16%)**.

Until you sell, your gain or loss is **"unrealized"** (it's on paper and can still change). When you sell, it becomes **"realized"** (it's locked in).

**Key idea:** Your profit or loss = (price now − price you paid) × number of shares.

## 5. Reading a stock quote and chart

When you look up a stock in the app, you'll see something like:

> **AAPL — Apple** · **$190.25** · **+2.10 (+1.12%) today**

- **$190.25** is the most recent price of **one share**.
- **+2.10 (+1.12%)** is how much it has moved since yesterday's close. Green/plus means up; red/minus means down.

The **chart** is a line showing the price over time. Left is the past, right is now. A line climbing from left to right means the price has risen over that period.

A few things to know:

- A stock going up recently does **not** mean it will keep going up.
- **Volatility** means how wildly a price jumps around. Tesla (TSLA) is usually much more volatile than Coca-Cola (KO). More volatility means bigger possible gains *and* bigger possible losses.

**Key idea:** The price is for one share. The chart shows history, not the future.

## 6. Order types: how to tell the broker what to do

An **order** is your instruction to buy or sell. This app supports three kinds:

### Market order: "Buy/sell right now"

You get the best price available at that moment. It's fast and almost always fills, but the exact price can be slightly different from what you saw a second ago.

> *Example:* AAPL shows $190. You place a market buy for 2 shares. You might pay $190.03 each.

### Limit order: "Only at this price or better"

You set the price.

- **Limit buy at $180** means: "Buy only if the price drops to $180 or lower."
- **Limit sell at $200** means: "Sell only if the price rises to $200 or higher."

If the price never gets there, nothing happens. You're never charged a worse price than your limit.

### Stop-loss (sell): "Get me out if it falls to this price"

This is a safety net for stock you already own.

> *Example:* You bought at $190. You set a stop-loss at $170. If the price falls to $170, the app automatically sells (as a market order). You lose about $20 a share instead of possibly much more.

In a fast crash the sale can happen a bit below your stop price.

**"Day" vs "Good till canceled":** A *day* order is canceled automatically if it hasn't filled by the end of the trading day. *Good till canceled (GTC)* keeps waiting until it fills or you cancel it.

**Key idea:** Market = now. Limit = my price or better. Stop-loss = automatic exit if things go wrong.

## 7. Your portfolio: understanding your numbers

Your **portfolio** is everything you own in the account. The app shows:

- **Cash:** Money not invested yet.
- **Position:** How many shares of one stock you own.
- **Average cost:** The average price you paid per share. (Buy 1 share at $100 and 1 at $120: your average cost is $110.)
- **Market value:** Shares × current price.
- **Unrealized P/L ("profit/loss"):** (current price − average cost) × shares.
- **Total account value:** Cash + market value of all your positions.

**Key idea:** Total value = cash + what your stocks are worth right now.

## 8. Risk: how to not get hurt

These rules matter more than anything else in this tutorial:

1. **Only invest money you won't need for at least 5 years.** Keep an emergency fund in a normal savings account first.
2. **Don't put everything in one stock.** If that one company has a bad year, so do you. Spreading money out is called **diversification**.
3. **Index funds/ETFs are the beginner's friend.** A fund like **SPY** holds 500 large US companies in a single share. If one company fails, it barely matters.
4. **Day trading (buying and selling within hours) is how most beginners lose money.** Studies of day traders consistently find that most lose money over time. Long-term investing is far more forgiving.
5. **Start small.** When you move to real money, start with an amount you'd be OK losing completely while you learn.
6. **Don't panic-sell.** Prices drop sometimes. Selling in a panic locks in the loss.
7. **Frequent trading rules exist.** In the US, brokers have long applied rules to people who day-trade often with small accounts (the "pattern day trader" rule). Check your broker's current rules before trading frequently.

**Key idea:** Your first job is to *not lose a lot*. Making money comes second.

## 9. Fees and taxes (the basics)

- **Commissions:** Many brokers, including Alpaca, charge **no commission** for normal US stock trades. Small regulatory fees can apply when you sell.
- **The spread:** There's always a small gap between the highest price buyers offer (the **bid**) and the lowest price sellers ask (the **ask**). Buying and immediately selling costs you that gap.
- **Taxes:** In most countries, profits from selling stock and dividends are taxable. Rules depend on where you live and how long you held the stock. Your broker gives you yearly tax documents. When in doubt, ask a tax professional.

**Key idea:** "Free" trading still has small costs, and profits are usually taxable.

## 10. Common mistakes and scams

**Mistakes:**

- **FOMO** (fear of missing out): buying something *because* it already shot up.
- **Going all in** on one "sure thing."
- **Trading on tips** from social media, chat groups, or friends.
- **Checking prices every minute** and reacting emotionally.
- **Not having a plan:** decide *before* you buy why you're buying and when you'd sell.

**Scam warning signs:**

- "Guaranteed" returns, or "you can't lose"
- Pressure to act **right now**
- "Signals" groups, paid "mentors," or someone you met online offering to manage your money or trade for you
- Requests to send money to a person, a crypto wallet, or any app other than your own regulated broker
- Anyone asking for your **API secret key** or password

This app never asks for your password and keeps your keys only on your own computer.

**Key idea:** If it sounds too good to be true, it is.

1. Make sure the top switch says **Practice** (green banner).
2. Go to the **Trade** tab.

4. Choose **Buy**, type **5** shares, leave the order type on **Market**.

6. Open the **Portfolio** tab. You now own 5 shares. Watch the P/L move as the price changes.
7. Try a **Limit buy** a little below the current price and watch it in the **Orders** tab. It fills only if the price drops to your limit.
8. Try a **Stop-loss** on your AAPL shares a little below the current price.
9. Sell your shares and see your realized gain or loss.

You can click **Reset practice account** on the Settings tab at any time.

### Moving to Paper trading (real prices, pretend money)

1. Go to **alpaca.markets** and sign up for free.
2. In the Alpaca dashboard, make sure you're on the **Paper** account. Find **API Keys** and click **Generate** (or **Regenerate**).
3. Copy the **Key ID** and **Secret Key**. The secret is shown only once.
4. In this app, open **Settings → Paper trading keys**, paste both, and click **Save & test**.
5. Switch the top selector to **Paper** (blue banner).

Practice in Paper mode for **at least a few weeks**. Remember: real orders only fill while the market is open.
